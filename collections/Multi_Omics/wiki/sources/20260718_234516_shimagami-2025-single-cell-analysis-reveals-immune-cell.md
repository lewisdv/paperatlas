# Single-cell analysis reveals immune cell abnormalities underlying the clinical heterogeneity of patients with systemic sclerosis

## Key findings

- Joint single-cell transcriptome and 43-surface-protein profiling of PBMCs from 21 immunosuppressive-treatment-naive systemic-sclerosis patients and six matched controls resolved organ-complication-associated immune states.
- EGR1+ CD14+ monocytes were associated with scleroderma renal crisis, activated NF-κB signaling, and differentiated toward tissue-damaging macrophages found at injury sites.
- A type-II-interferon-signature CD8+ T-cell subset appeared in blood and lung tissue from patients with progressive interstitial lung disease, supporting chemokine-linked tissue migration.
- The study links clinical heterogeneity to distinct immune-cell programs rather than a single disease-wide signature.

## Methods and interpretation

- 10x single-cell profiling with paired surface-protein measurements, clinical stratification, tissue validation, and functional differentiation analyses.
- Associations support biomarker and mechanism hypotheses but do not by themselves establish prospective predictive performance.
