# Morphological diversification and functional maturation of human astrocytes in glia-enriched cortical organoid transplanted in mouse brain

## Source

- DOI: [10.1038/s41587-024-02157-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-024-02157-8)
- PMID: 38418648
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233522_wang-2024-morphological-diversification-and-functional-maturation.pdf)

## Key findings

- A timed gliogenic switch using PDGF-AA and astrocyte-supporting medium generated glia-enriched cortical organoids with approximately 25–31% astroglial cells by 8–10 weeks while retaining neurogenic capacity.
- The model also produced cortical excitatory and inhibitory neurons and oligodendrocyte progenitor cells.
- After mouse-brain transplantation, human astrocytes diversified into anatomical morphologies and showed enhanced transcriptomic maturation relative to in-vitro cortical-organoid astrocytes.
- Spatial transcriptomics and morphological analyses linked host cortical location with astrocyte diversification.

## Relevance

- Demonstrates that lineage timing can be deliberately rebalanced to study a cell type underrepresented by standard cortical organoids.
- Supports transplantation as a maturation context while keeping the host contribution explicit.

## Limitations

- Host-derived signals drive part of the maturation, so graft phenotypes cannot be attributed to an autonomous organoid program alone.
- Accelerated gliogenesis may change neuron–glia developmental timing relative to native cortex.
