# FABP7 controls radial glial scaffold stability during human cortical development

## Source

- DOI: [10.1073/pnas.2523130123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2523130123)
- PMID: 41984827
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233522_wang-2026-fabp7-controls-radial-glial-scaffold.pdf)

## Key findings

- Human fetal slices, mouse embryonic cortex, CRISPRi cerebral organoids, and assembloids converged on a requirement for FABP7 in radial-glial scaffold integrity and neuronal positioning.
- FABP7 loss disrupted cytoskeletal, neural-fate, and stress-response programs and produced scaffold phenotypes overlapping idiopathic-autism organoid signatures.
- Mechanistically, FABP7 deficiency suppressed the mevalonate pathway and altered small-GTPase/cytoskeletal organization.
- Mevalonic-acid rescue mitigated structural and migration defects, supporting a FABP7–MVA–GTPase causal axis.

## Relevance

- Connects metabolic state to a physical developmental scaffold, linking molecular perturbation with migration geometry.
- Multi-model convergence strengthens the organoid result by comparison with primary human tissue and in-vivo mouse cortex.

## Limitations

- Cross-model agreement does not remove species and perturbation-method differences.
- Transcriptomic convergence with autism is disease relevance, not proof that FABP7 deficiency is a common causal mechanism across ASD.
