# Human microglia in brain assembloids display region-specific diversity and respond to hyperexcitable neurons carrying SCN2A mutation

## Source

- DOI: [10.1126/sciadv.ady2977](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.ady2977)
- PMID: 41706855
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233522_wu-2026-human-microglia-in-brain-assembloids.pdf)

## Key findings

- Common hiPSC-derived microglia integrated into cortical, striatal, and midbrain organoids and acquired six transcriptional subtypes with region-biased distributions.
- Striatal-organoid microglia were enriched for GABA-receptor programs, including GABAB-receptor-associated signatures.
- Microglia-containing midbrain–striatal assembloids supported axonal projections and chemogenetically evoked microglial calcium responses involving GABAB receptors.
- In SCN2A-C959X assembloids, neuronal hyperactivity drove elevated microglial calcium responses and excessive synaptic pruning; pharmacological GABAB inhibition or microglial GABBR1 knockout reversed these effects.

## Relevance

- Shows that microglial state is conditioned by regional neural context rather than being a single portable cell type.
- Connects region identity, circuit activity, immune calcium signaling, and synaptic pruning in a perturbable assembloid platform.

## Limitations

- In-vitro regional signatures and short-term rescue do not establish adult human microglial identity or durable therapeutic benefit.
- GABAB dependence was demonstrated in this SCN2A circuit context and should not be generalized to all neurodevelopmental disorders.
