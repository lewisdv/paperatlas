# Suspension culture promotes serosal mesothelial development in human intestinal organoids

## Source

- DOI: [10.1016/j.celrep.2022.110379](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2022.110379)
- PMID: 35172130
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233522_capeling-2022-suspension-culture-promotes-serosal-mesothelial.pdf)

## Key findings

- hPSC-derived human intestinal organoids could be maintained in suspension without a 3D ECM or hydrogel scaffold.
- Suspension culture promoted an organized serosal mesothelial layer resembling primary fetal intestinal serosa by histology and scRNA-seq.
- Organoid serosal cells showed fibrinolytic activity and capacity to generate smooth-muscle-like cells.
- An inhibitor screen implicated Hedgehog and WNT signaling in human serosal mesothelial differentiation and patterning.

## Relevance

- Demonstrates that removing exogenous matrix can reveal endogenous mesenchymal organization while reducing cost and xenogeneic variability.
- Adds a serosal compartment to [gastrointestinal and endodermal organoid systems](../concepts/gastrointestinal-and-endodermal-organoid-systems.md).

## Limitations

- Fetal-like transcriptional similarity and selected functions do not establish a complete mature serosa.
- Suspension changes multiple mechanical and diffusional variables, so the direct cause of enhanced mesothelium is not isolated.
