# Complex oscillatory waves emerging from cortical organoids model early human brain network development

## Source

- DOI: [10.1016/j.stem.2019.08.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2019.08.002)
- PMID: 31474560
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233522_trujillo-2019-complex-oscillatory-waves-emerging-from.pdf)

## Key findings

- Longitudinal cortical organoids progressed from progenitor-rich states toward neuronal and glial populations over approximately 10 months.
- Network activity emerged as synchronous events around two months, developed 2–3 Hz rhythmicity by four to six months, and later became more spatiotemporally variable as inhibitory populations developed.
- Oscillations depended on glutamatergic and GABAergic signaling and displayed cross-frequency coupling.
- A model trained on preterm neonatal EEG features mapped aspects of organoid electrophysiological development onto a preterm-like trajectory.

## Relevance

- Establishes longitudinal network dynamics as a maturation axis alongside cell identity and gene expression.
- Anchors [MEA electrophysiology readouts](../entities/mea-electrophysiology-readouts.md) and calcium/network assays used in later disease organoids.

## Limitations

- Similarity in selected EEG-derived features does not mean organoids reproduce a preterm brain or conscious state.
- Culture-specific architecture, missing sensory inputs, and immature glia constrain network interpretation.
