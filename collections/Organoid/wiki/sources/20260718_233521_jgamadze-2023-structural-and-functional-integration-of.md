# Structural and functional integration of human forebrain organoids with the injured adult rat visual system

## Source

- DOI: [10.1016/j.stem.2023.01.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2023.01.004)
- PMID: 36736289
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233521_jgamadze-2023-structural-and-functional-integration-of.pdf)

## Key findings

- Day-80–88 forebrain organoids transplanted into full-thickness adult rat visual-cortex cavities survived, grew, and became vascularized by host vessels.
- Viral trans-synaptic tracing supported reciprocal graft-host connectivity and a polysynaptic route from host retina through the visual system to graft neurons.
- High-density electrophysiology detected spontaneous graft activity and visually evoked responses, including orientation-selective units.
- The study therefore moves transplantation evidence beyond histological survival to systems-level sensory integration in an injured adult brain.

## Methods

- hPSC-derived cortical organoids generated with dual-SMAD inhibition and transplanted into aspiration lesions in immunosuppressed adult rats.
- Histology, anterograde and retrograde viral tracing, laminar probes, local field potentials, and single-unit visual-response analysis.

## Relevance

- Strong evidence for the escalation step from in-vitro maturation to [host-context transplantation and repair validation](../entities/host-context-transplantation-and-repair-validation.md).
- Complements the collection's transplantation and vascularization evidence by requiring afferent/efferent connectivity and task-relevant physiology, not graft survival alone.

## Limitations

- Xenograft immunosuppression, rodent visual circuitry, short follow-up, and incomplete cortical architecture limit direct therapeutic inference.
- Orientation selectivity demonstrates integration but not restoration of a lost behavioral function.
