# Human adherent cortical organoids in a multi-well format

## Source

- DOI: [10.7554/eLife.98340](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.98340)
- PMID: 42083454
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233521_van-der-kroeg-2026-human-adherent-cortical-organoids-in.pdf)

## Key findings

- Frontal-cortex-patterned iPSC neural progenitors self-organized in 384-well plates into adherent cortical organoids with a stereotyped approximately 3 × 3 × 0.2 mm geometry.
- The thin adherent format produced standardized radial cortical structures while reducing the internal necrosis and patch-to-patch topographic variability of free-floating organoids.
- Cultures contained neuronal subtypes, astrocytes, and oligodendrocyte-lineage cells and were maintained for at least 10 months.
- Longitudinal imaging showed dendritic spines, axonal myelination, and robust neuronal activity, supporting screening-oriented use.

## Relevance

- A concrete reproducibility/throughput design for [organoid engineering, imaging, and screening](../concepts/organoid-engineering-imaging-and-screening.md).
- Separates geometric standardization and oxygen diffusion from biological patterning as independent levers for assay reliability.

## Limitations

- The flattened adherent geometry intentionally departs from unconstrained 3D morphogenesis.
- Reproducibility and long culture do not establish adult cortical equivalence or full in-vivo cellular diversity.
