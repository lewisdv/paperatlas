# Differentiation of human intestinal organoids with endogenous vascular endothelial cells

## Source

- DOI: [10.1016/j.devcel.2020.07.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2020.07.023)
- PMID: 32841595
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233522_holloway-2020-differentiation-of-human-intestinal-organoids.pdf)

## Key findings

- scRNA-seq identified an endogenous endothelial population early in hPSC intestinal-organoid differentiation that normally declines with culture time.
- Modified culture conditions expanded and maintained this population in vascularized HIOs (vHIOs) without adding an independently derived endothelial lineage.
- Bulk and single-cell comparisons with developing human intestine, lung, and kidney showed that HIO endothelial cells most closely matched intestine-enriched endothelial signatures.
- The work supports co-differentiation as an alternative to post hoc endothelial coculture or host-only vascularization after transplantation.

## Relevance

- Important evidence for organ-specific endothelial identity in [organoid vascularization and perfusion strategies](../concepts/organoid-vascularization-and-perfusion-strategies.md).
- Shows why lineage presence should be validated by tissue-specific transcriptomic identity rather than generic endothelial markers alone.

## Limitations

- Endothelial identity does not demonstrate a perfused vascular network or barrier function.
- In-vitro vHIOs still lack other native intestinal components and mature physiology.
