# PU.1 restores microglial dysfunction caused by C9ORF72 repeat expansions in neural organoids

## Source

- DOI: [10.1093/brain/awaf340](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awaf340)
- PMID: 40966720
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233522_ljubikj-2025-pu1-restores-microglial-dysfunction-caused.pdf)

## Key findings

- Cerebral organoids from C9ORF72-repeat-expansion ALS/FTD iPSCs innately developed microglia within a complex neural environment.
- C9 organoid-derived microglia showed reduced morphological complexity and dysregulation of phagocytic, lysosomal, and immune-response programs.
- Functional assays found impaired particle phagocytosis, reduced PSD-95 engulfment, lower LAMP1, and reduced inflammatory-cue release.
- PU.1/SPI1 was the most downregulated inferred regulon; viral PU.1 expression rescued phagocytosis and part of the gene-expression defect.

## Relevance

- Provides a disease-context example in which a transcriptomically inferred regulator is tested by rescue rather than reported as correlation alone.
- Adds microglial lysosome/phagocytosis readouts to brain-organoid disease-model design.

## Limitations

- PU.1 overexpression is a mechanistic rescue, not evidence of a safe therapeutic window.
- Patient-line variability and organoid microglial maturity constrain generalization to adult ALS/FTD microglia.
