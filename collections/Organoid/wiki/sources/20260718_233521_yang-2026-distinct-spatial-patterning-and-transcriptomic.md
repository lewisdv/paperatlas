# Distinct spatial patterning and transcriptomic landscapes of human neural organoids by localized delivery of morphogens

## Source

- DOI: [10.1016/j.stem.2026.01.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2026.01.008)
- PMID: 41734763
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233521_yang-2026-distinct-spatial-patterning-and-transcriptomic.pdf)

## Key findings

- A Matrigel-free passive-diffusion morphogen-gradient generator (PdMG) creates steep, localized gradients within neural organoids.
- SHH/WNT inhibition, WNT, and retinoic-acid gradients produced dorsal–ventral forebrain, forebrain–midbrain, and forebrain–hindbrain-like axes, respectively.
- Spatial transcriptomics showed early regionalization followed by neurogenesis and GABAergic interneuron migration in later patterned organoids.
- The platform models morphogen position and time within one tissue rather than generating uniform regional identities or fusing separately patterned organoids.

## Methods

- Local morphogen delivery by passive diffusion, immunophenotyping, spatial transcriptomics, and longitudinal analysis of early and late organoids.

## Relevance

- Extends [brain organoid patterning and assembloids](../concepts/brain-organoid-patterning-and-assembloids.md) with an axis-building strategy that preserves continuous spatial context.
- Useful for experiments where boundary formation and migration across a gradient are the causal variables.

## Limitations

- Engineered gradients simplify the multiple interacting signals and changing tissue mechanics of embryonic development.
- Regional transcriptomic identity does not by itself establish mature connectivity or physiological equivalence.
