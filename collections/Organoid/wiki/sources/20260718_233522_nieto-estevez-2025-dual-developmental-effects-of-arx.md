# Dual developmental effects of ARX poly-alanine mutations on human cortical excitatory and inhibitory neurons

## Source

- DOI: [10.1016/j.celrep.2025.116746](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2025.116746)
- PMID: 41422506
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233522_nieto-estevez-2025-dual-developmental-effects-of-arx.pdf)

## Key findings

- Patient-derived cortical and ganglionic-eminence organoids modeled ARX poly-alanine expansion mutations associated with infantile spasms.
- Mutations increased cortical progenitor proliferation and accelerated early cortical development, with context- and time-dependent ARX expression changes.
- Interneuron migration was enhanced cell-autonomously and was rescued by CXCR4 inhibition.
- Cortical–ganglionic-eminence assembloids developed early network hyperactivity, linking lineage-specific developmental changes to circuit phenotype.

## Relevance

- Demonstrates why dorsal and ventral compartments must be evaluated separately before fusion: the same mutation produced distinct excitatory-progenitor and inhibitory-migration effects.
- Adds developmental epilepsy to the collection's disease-assembloid evidence.

## Limitations

- Patient lines lacked isogenic controls, although effects were reported across lines.
- CXCR4 rescue identifies a tractable migration mechanism but does not establish long-term seizure correction or clinical efficacy.
