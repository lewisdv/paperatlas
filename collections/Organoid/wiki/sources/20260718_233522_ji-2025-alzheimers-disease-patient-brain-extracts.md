# Alzheimer’s disease patient brain extracts induce multiple pathologies in novel vascularized neuroimmune organoids

## Source

- DOI: [10.1038/s41380-025-03041-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-025-03041-w)
- PMID: 40316675
- Raw PDF: [source](../../raw/sources/20260718_233522_ji-2025-alzheimers-disease-patient-brain-extracts.pdf)

## Key findings

- Neural, primitive macrophage, and vascular progenitors were co-assembled into organoids containing neurons, astrocytes, microglia, and vascular-like structures.
- Sporadic-AD postmortem brain extracts induced Aβ plaque-like and tau tangle-like aggregates, neuroinflammation, excessive microglial synaptic pruning, synapse/neuron loss, and impaired network activity within four weeks.
- Proteomics identified disruption of AD-related pathways in the exposed organoids.
- Lecanemab reduced amyloid burden but increased vascular inflammatory responses, illustrating simultaneous efficacy and safety readouts in a multi-lineage model.

## Relevance

- Demonstrates how [multi-lineage and tissue complexity](../concepts/multi-lineage-and-tissue-complexity.md) can support a sporadic-disease trigger that is absent from mutation-only organoid models.
- Connects vascular, immune, proteostatic, and functional readouts in one test system.

## Limitations

- Brain extracts are a complex, incompletely controlled exposure and do not reconstruct the chronic origin of sporadic AD.
- Vascular-like cells and short-term pathology induction should not be equated with a mature blood–brain barrier or decades-long disease progression.
