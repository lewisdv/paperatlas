# Kidney organoid differentiation routes

## Current position in this corpus

Kidney protocols in this collection are less about whether organoids are possible and more about which developmental route and segment balance should be prioritized.

## Working synthesis

- Xia emphasizes a ureteric-bud-oriented route and chimeric assembly logic.
- Takasato pushes toward a broader kidney model that includes several renal progenitor compartments.
- Morizane and Vanslambrouck illustrate the newer move toward efficiency and segment-biased refinement rather than a single generic kidney organoid recipe.
- Pecksen 2024 adds a different optimization axis: monocyte or macrophage support can rescue the CHIR-stressed early differentiation window, implying that some kidney failures are support-compartment problems rather than route-choice problems.
- Pou Casellas 2026 shows that protocol choice should be benchmarked at cell-state resolution: organoids, tissue-derived tubuloids, and organoid-derived tubuloids differ in adaptive states and segment maturity, while distal/connecting/intercalated populations remain scarce across models.
- The practical implication is to select against the required nephron segment and function, not a generic claim that one model is globally more mature.

## Main tension

- broad renal complexity versus cleaner progenitor control
- general kidney identity versus segment-enriched optimization
- lineage recipe optimization versus support-cell rescue during early differentiation

## Open questions

- Which kidney differentiation route best matches the disease or screening question at hand?
- How much specialization is worth the tradeoff in developmental breadth or compatibility with older protocols?
- Which non-renal support cells or secreted factors most improve robustness without blurring kidney identity?
