# Long-horizon brain-organoid maturation and temporal memory

## Core distinction

Long-term culture is not itself evidence of mature or faithful tissue. This corpus treats it as a testable claim with three separable layers: temporal correspondence to an endogenous reference, maintenance of functional cell populations, and preserved developmental ordering of progenitor fate potential.

## Evidence in this corpus

- [Gordon 2021](../sources/gordon_2021_longterm_maturation_human_cortical_organoids_postnatal_transitions.md) reports a directed cortical-spheroid fetal-to-postnatal transition at about 250–300 days, supported by transcriptomic mapping, DNA-methylation age, RNA editing, and HDAC/NMDA-subunit switches. It also identifies activity-dependent and non-cortical programs as residual gaps.
- [Faravelli 2026](../sources/faravelli_2026_human_brain_organoids_record_time_years.md) follows cortical organoids to five years, adds single-cell reference mapping and whole-genome methylation across the extended span, maintains functional excitatory networks to two years under APM, and tests temporal ordering by heterochronic reaggregation.

## Practical measurement frame

- **Temporal correspondence:** compare cell-type-resolved transcriptomic modules and epigenomic clocks with declared endogenous references; report the reference and the temporal range, rather than treating one score as universal age.
- **Cell-population maintenance:** assess whether targeted populations remain represented, structurally mature, and free of increasing stress signatures. This is especially important where composition can shift while an average organoid score still rises.
- **Function:** pair molecular trajectories with direct readouts such as synapse structure and MEA burst dynamics. A long culture duration does not substitute for a functional assay.
- **Temporal memory:** distinguish continued maturation from a fate-potential experiment. Heterochronic progenitor assays ask whether previously aged cells can respond to new signals while retaining a record of developmental steps already completed.

## Boundaries and tensions

- Reference-aligned postnatal signatures do not establish complete adult tissue equivalence, intact vascular/non-neural context, or every activity-dependent program.
- Culture conditions that improve survival or activity can change composition and molecular state. They should be compared as interventions, not presumed to reveal a condition-independent intrinsic clock.
- A bulk or organoid-level age value can conceal divergent cell-type trajectories; single-cell or otherwise compartment-aware analysis is central when the question is temporal fidelity.
