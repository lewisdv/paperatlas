# Human fetal brain reference atlases

## Current position

Primary fetal references are essential for assigning developmental stage, region, and fidelity to disease models. Dissociated and spatial atlases answer complementary questions.

## Anchor sources

- [Eze 2021](../sources/20260718_234948_eze-2021-single-cell-atlas-of-early-human.md) emphasizes first-trimester whole-brain progenitor heterogeneity and cross-species/organoid comparison.
- [Qian 2025](../sources/20260718_234948_qian-2025-spatial-transcriptomics-reveals-human-cortical.md) preserves cortical position across layers, areas, and gestational stages and distinguishes gradient-like from sharp areal boundaries.

## Working rules

- Match the reference to the modeled developmental window; later neuronal agreement cannot repair missing early neuroepithelial states.
- Use dissociated atlases for broad cell-state resolution and spatial atlases for layer, boundary, and neighborhood claims.
- Keep measured genes/cells separate from imputed or computationally transferred labels.

## Open questions

- Which early human progenitor states remain systematically absent across current in-vitro models?
- How stable are areal identities across donors, developmental time, and assay platforms?
