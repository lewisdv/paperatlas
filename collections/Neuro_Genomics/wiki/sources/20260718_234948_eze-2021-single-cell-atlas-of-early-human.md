# Single-cell atlas of early human brain development highlights heterogeneity of human neuroepithelial cells and early radial glia

## Key findings

- scRNA-seq profiled approximately 289,000 cells from first-trimester human brain regions, including telencephalon, diencephalon, midbrain, hindbrain, cerebellum, ganglionic eminences, thalamus, hypothalamus, and cortex.
- Nine progenitor populations near the telencephalon revealed early neuroepithelial/radial-glial heterogeneity, including a prevalent mesenchymal-like population that declined when neurogenesis began.
- Human–mouse comparison identified two progenitor clusters enriched in early human cortical development.
- Organoid comparisons showed low fidelity to the earliest neuroepithelial and early radial-glial states, with improved agreement after neurogenesis progressed.

## Interpretation and limitations

- This is a primary developmental reference atlas and a benchmark for identifying which developmental windows an in-vitro model does or does not capture.
- Cross-sectional fetal sampling, dissociation effects, uneven regional availability, and species-stage alignment limit trajectory and conservation claims.
- The organoid conclusion is stage-specific: low early fidelity should not be generalized to all later organoid cell types or protocols.
