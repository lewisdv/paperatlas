# Single-cell analysis of Foxp1-driven mechanisms essential for striatal development

## Key findings

- Cell-type-specific Foxp1 deletion in direct-pathway, indirect-pathway, or both spiny projection neuron populations showed that Foxp1 regulates striatal cellular composition, neurochemical organization, and connectivity.
- scRNA-seq resolved distinct Foxp1 target programs in neuronal subtypes and exposed non-cell-autonomous compensation in populations that were not directly perturbed.
- Foxp1 was especially important for indirect-pathway SPN specification and striosome–matrix compartment organization.
- Molecular changes were connected with motor learning, ultrasonic vocalization, fear-conditioning, and other circuit/behavior phenotypes relevant to human FOXP1 loss-of-function disorders.

## Methods

- Conditional mouse genetics, early postnatal striatal scRNA-seq, serial two-photon tomography, molecular validation, and behavioral assays.

## Interpretation and limitations

- The study demonstrates how an NDD-associated transcription factor can have cell-type-specific and non-cell-autonomous effects within one circuit.
- It is a mouse developmental mechanism study; relevance to human FOXP1 syndrome requires human cell/tissue validation and should not be inferred from behavioral similarity alone.
