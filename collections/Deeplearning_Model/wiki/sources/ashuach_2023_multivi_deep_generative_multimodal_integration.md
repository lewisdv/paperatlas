## Summary

MultiVI is a probabilistic deep generative model for integrating paired multiome cells with RNA-only, ATAC-only, and optionally protein-only cells. It creates a shared low-dimensional cell-state representation and a normalized high-dimensional view of every modeled modality, including unobserved modalities generated by the model. MultiVI extends scVI, PeakVI, and totalVI with modality-specific encoders/decoders, explicit latent-space alignment, batch correction, uncertainty-aware imputation, and Bayesian differential analysis. It is distributed through `scvi-tools`.

## Methods

- RNA, accessibility, and protein are encoded separately into Gaussian latent distributions.
- Paired-cell latent distributions are aligned with symmetric KL penalties and averaged; unpaired cells use the latent distribution of their observed modality.
- RNA uses a negative-binomial likelihood, ATAC a Bernoulli likelihood, and protein a foreground/background mixture inherited from totalVI.
- Modality-specific decoders generate normalized or imputed high-dimensional features.
- Adversarial domain adaptation reduces batch or technology separation in the shared latent space.
- Imputation uncertainty is estimated by repeated samples from the latent distribution.

## Key Claims

- A relatively small paired multiome set can anchor much larger single-modality collections in one joint representation.
- Missing modalities can be imputed with uncertainty estimates and used for differential analysis, not only visualization.
- The modular generative design extends from RNA+ATAC to RNA+ATAC+protein and mixed modality combinations.

## Evidence

- Estimated library-size factors correlate with observed RNA and ATAC depths at `0.97` and `0.91`.
- On held-out PBMC modalities, accessibility imputation has AUPRC `0.41`; against independently smoothed observations, Spearman correlations are `0.80` for accessibility and `0.86` for expression.
- When accessibility is removed from a perturbed cell population, reconstructed accessibility has Spearman `0.93`.
- Held-out B-cell differential effects correlate with observations at `0.57` for all expression features (`0.74` for genes with observed logFC above 1) and `0.67` for accessibility.
- Of significant model-derived results, `75%` of differentially expressed genes and `86%` of differentially accessible regions are recovered in the held-out data at 5% FDR.
- In the tri-modal experiment, imputed expression has Spearman `0.78`, accessibility `0.73-0.76`, and foreground protein abundance `0.53`, depending on the observed input modality.

## Limitations

- Much of the missingness benchmark is created by artificially unpairing paired datasets, which is cleaner than many real mosaic collections.
- The authors explicitly caution that small datasets may not contain enough observations to train neural networks and identify multiple modality-specific embeddings.
- Imputation quality varies substantially by feature and population; uncertainty is useful but does not convert generated values into measurements.
- Expression-imputation uncertainty is confounded by baseline gene abundance, making calibration harder to interpret than for accessibility.
- The main demonstrations are blood-focused, with no direct experimental validation of imputed molecular states.

## Open Questions

- How much paired data is needed as modality number and cell-state diversity grow?
- Which uncertainty calibration tests best distinguish biological absence from technical dropout?
- When are differential analyses on imputed features reliable enough to guide experiments?
