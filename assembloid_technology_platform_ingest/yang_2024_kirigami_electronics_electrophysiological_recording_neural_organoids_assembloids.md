## Abstract
Realizing the potential of organoids and assembloids to model neural development and disease requires improved methods for long-term, minimally invasive recording of electrical activity. Existing technologies (patch clamp, penetrating microelectrodes, planar electrode arrays, substrate-attached flexible electrodes) do not allow chronic recording of organoids in suspension, which is necessary to preserve architecture. Inspired by kirigami art, the authors developed flexible electronics that transition from a 2D to a 3D basket-like configuration with either spiral or honeycomb patterns to accommodate long-term culture of organoids in suspension. This platform, named kirigami electronics (KiriE), integrates with and enables chronic recording of cortical organoids for up to 120 days while preserving their morphology, cytoarchitecture and cell composition. KiriE integrates with optogenetic and pharmacological manipulation and models phenotypes related to a genetic disease, and can capture corticostriatal connectivity in assembloids following optogenetic stimulation. KiriE thus enables investigation of disease and activity patterns underlying nervous system assembly.

## Key findings
- **32-channel 3D "basket" MEA that self-folds in suspension.** Ultrathin (~0.9 µm total, two SU-8 insulation layers around metal) 2D kirigami precursor spontaneously transforms into a 1-cm-diameter 3D basket carrying **32 microelectrodes (25 µm diameter)** clustered in a **1 mm central area**; central SU-8/metal interconnects are 9 µm / 3 µm wide. Two patterns fabricated: spiral (concentric rings + spiral latches) and honeycomb (serpentine-lined hexagonal tiles).
- **Mechanically optimized to stay far below SU-8's elastic limit.** FEM (nonlinear Naghdi-shell, FEniCS) predicts a maximum strain of **0.06% (spiral)** and **0.026% (honeycomb)** under self-weight plus a 1.2-mm organoid in medium — well below the ~2–4% SU-8 elastic limit. Strain is minimized precisely between the first and second rings where the organoid sits. Spiral chosen for routine use (shorter routing ~8 mm vs ~17 mm honeycomb → fewer defects; easier centering).
- **Chronic integration up to 120 days without flattening.** hCOs placed on KiriE at **day 20**, followed to **day 190** (170 days post-integration). KiriE embeds **300–500 µm** deep by day 26; FIB-SEM shows electrodes in close cell contact at ~180 µm depth. hCOs on KiriE keep spherical morphology/circularity over 44 days, whereas hCOs on an adhesive substrate flatten to **100–200 µm** thickness within 28 days and show higher dead-cell (SYTOX) and cleaved-caspase-3 signal — establishing suspension culture as critical for 3D preservation and viability.
- **KiriE does not perturb corticogenesis (scRNA-seq).** At day 100 (40 days post-integration), **25,546 cells / n=4 samples / 2 hiPSC lines** resolve 7 clusters with cell-type proportions matched between KiriE-integrated and control hCOs (glutamatergic neurons SLC17A7/SLC17A6 66.59%, radial glia MOXD1 11.85%, astroglia AQP4 5.26%, proliferating TOP2A 5.00%, intermediate progenitors EOMES 3.46%); high Pearson correlation of normalized expression. SOX2+ ventricular zones, MAP2+ neurons and GFAP+ glia remain spatially uniform at the interface.
- **Months-long single-unit recording; activity onsets ~day 100.** Spontaneous activity recorded from **day 75 to day 179**; none detected at day 75, notable activity from day 96, with network activity arising at **day 100 ± 5**. A single electrode can resolve up to **4 neurons**; spike amplitudes **30–140 µV**, stable across days. Electrode impedance stable at **~0.3 MΩ**. Firing rates matched acutely inserted silicon-shank probes (days 116–125), cross-validating the platform.
- **Optogenetic stimulation drives ~20× firing increase.** hCOs expressing hSyn1::ChrimsonR-tdTomato stimulated with 590 nm light (1 s pulse / 10 s) via a fiber cannula from beneath the chamber. Light-on firing **5.44 ± 0.57 Hz** vs light-off **0.24 ± 0.03 Hz** (~20×; ****P = 3.69 × 10⁻¹⁴; n=43 channels, 2 hCOs, 32 pulses).
- **Pharmacology via built-in perfusion, bidirectional and reversible.** 4-AP (K⁺ blocker, 50 µM) increased firing ~3× (**0.49 ± 0.08 → 1.60 ± 0.25 Hz**; ****P = 4.31 × 10⁻⁵, n=67 channels, 3 hCOs); TTX (Na⁺ blocker, 1 µM) nearly abolished activity (**P = 1.83 × 10⁻³**), reversible on washout.
- **Detects a 22q11.2/ASD-schizophrenia disease phenotype.** DGCR8⁺/⁻ hCOs (heterozygous loss; 22q11.2 deletion-region gene) showed ~3× higher spontaneous firing than isogenic control (**1.01 ± 0.16 Hz**, days 133–141, n=70 channels/4 hCOs vs **0.38 ± 0.04 Hz**, days 137–138, n=51 channels/2 hCOs; **P = 1.2 × 10⁻³**). **~30% of DGCR8⁺/⁻ channels fired >1 Hz vs 2% in controls**, some up to ~7 Hz — recapitulating prior calcium-imaging/patch-clamp hyperexcitability without dissociation or slicing.
- **Captures functional corticostriatal circuits in in-situ assembloids.** hStrO placed at organoid center, hCO added adjacent 2 days later to fuse on the device. eYFP+ cortical projections invade hStrO by ~30 days post-assembly; minimal reverse mCherry+ striatal projection into hCO (consistent with unilateral corticostriatal wiring in vivo). Channel-indexing barcodes assigned electrodes to each compartment (example: 20 in hStrO, 7 in hCO).
- **Optogenetic activation of cortex evokes downstream striatal firing.** ChrimsonR only in hCO neurons; 590 nm stimulation raised hStrO firing from light-off **0.08 ± 0.05 Hz** to light-on **0.77 ± 0.29 Hz** (*P = 2.04 × 10⁻², n=74 channels, 3 assembloids, 18–60 pulses). Light-evoked striatal responses emerged only **after ~7 weeks** post-assembly, tracking circuit maturation longitudinally.
- **Multimodal, reusable, in-incubator platform.** 3D-printed glass-bottom chamber supports simultaneous live confocal imaging, media perfusion, in-situ electrophysiology, and optogenetic/pharmacological modulation without transferring the culture; KiriE devices can be enzymatically cleaned and reused.

## Methods
- **Design/simulation:** Patterns scripted in Python (PHIDL); spiral latches built from 4-point NURBS curves for smooth (twice-differentiable) connections; interconnect routing solved as node-disjoint paths via NetworkX Edmonds–Karp max-flow. Mechanics: geometry meshed with Gmsh/pygmsh (~80,000 triangles), treated as a 2D shell in 3D (thickness ~10,000× smaller than lateral dims); nonlinear Naghdi-shell FEM in FEniCS/FEniCS-Shells; execution scales as N², limiting patterns to <100,000 triangles.
- **Fabrication:** Multilayer photolithography on 100-mm Si with 80-nm Ni sacrificial layer; Ti/Au (5/100 nm) I/O pads; interconnect Ti/Au/Pt (5/100/50 nm); insulation from SU-8 2000.5 (diluted 4:1 in cyclopentanone), fluorescent variant doped with Lissamine rhodamine B; final interdiffusion hard bake 195 °C 1.5 h. Channel-indexing barcodes for electrode ID. Released in FeCl3/HCl Ni etchant onto a PDMS support ring; direct-contact interfacing to a flat flexible cable (FFC).
- **Culture chamber:** 3D-printed PLA multifunctional chamber with glass coverslip bottom, inlet/outlet perfusion channels, FFC mount (Metabond); laminin-coated (overnight) to promote KiriE adhesion; syringe-pump perfusion from day 2 post-integration.
- **Organoids:** hCOs and hStrOs from hiPSCs per Paşca/Yoon/Miura protocols (dual-SMAD inhibition dorsomorphin + SB-431542; hStrO patterning with IWP-2, activin A, SR11237, later DAPT). Lines: 0524-1, DGCR8⁺/⁻, 8858-3, 1208-2, 8119-1, 1205-4, KOLF2.1J, LMNB1-mEGFP, plus a CAG::eGFP reporter line; 7 control lines from 7 healthy donors.
- **Viral tools:** AAV-DJ-hSyn1::eYFP, AAV-DJ-hSyn1::mCherry, AAV1-Syn::ChrimsonR-tdTomato.
- **Electrophysiology:** Intan RHS 32-channel stim/recording headstage via custom PCB; stainless-steel reference; **20 kHz** sampling; working channels defined by 1 kHz impedance between ~0.1 and ~0.8 MΩ. Spike detection: third-order Butterworth bandpass **150–9,000 Hz** (zero-phase filtfilt), 5-SD threshold on 3-ms windows, UMAP + DBSCAN clustering, z-normalized template matching (stumpy); firing rate via Gaussian-kernel convolution of the spike raster. Optogenetics: 590 nm, 1 s pulse/10 s train (Cyclops LED driver + Arduino). Pharmacology: 4-AP 50 µM, TTX 1 µM, NBQX 20 µM, APV 50 µM by perfusion with washout. Acute cross-validation with 32-ch Cambridge NeuroTech silicon shank on Intan 1024-ch controller.
- **Validation assays:** live confocal (Leica Stellaris 5), FIB-SEM (ultrathin plasticization; Helios CX5), SEM, SYTOX/cleaved-caspase-3 viability, immunostaining (SOX2, MAP2, GFAP, TBR1, SATB2, neurofilament), RT-qPCR (day 25), and 10x Genomics 3′ v3.1 scRNA-seq (Cell Ranger 7.0.0, Seurat 4.1.1; QC 1,000–9,000 genes, <10% mito; top-21 PCs, resolution 0.6).
- **Statistics:** two-tailed t-tests or one-way ANOVA with multiple comparisons; P < 0.05 significant (OriginLab).

## Relevance to the brain-organoid ASD review
- **Directly answers the "MEA / electrophysiology is poorly standardized" limitation.** KiriE supplies a reproducible, non-invasive, months-long single-unit + network electrophysiology readout on intact organoids in suspension — an infrastructure piece for deployable functional endpoints rather than one-off acute recordings.
- **Enables true longitudinal circuit phenotyping.** The same organoid/assembloid can be recorded from day 75 → 179 with stable impedance (~0.3 MΩ) and spike amplitudes (30–140 µV), letting studies track how activity emerges and diverges over development — the natural axis for ASD/neurodevelopmental trajectory questions.
- **Demonstrates a concrete ASD-relevant genetic readout.** The DGCR8⁺/⁻ (22q11.2) hyperexcitability result (~3× firing; 30% vs 2% of channels >1 Hz) is a template for E–I-imbalance / network-hyperactivity endpoints that recur across autism and schizophrenia models.
- **Extends functional readouts from organoids to assembloids.** Barcoded per-compartment recording plus cortex→striatum optogenetic driving shows the platform can quantify inter-regional circuit assembly (and its ~7-week maturation) — the level at which many ASD circuit hypotheses live.
- **Companion to Li 2022 stretchable mesh microelectronics (same Cui/Pașca collaboration; ref. 27).** Li 2022 provides substrate-integrated stretchable mesh recording/stimulation of organoids; KiriE advances this to fully suspended, deformable 3D baskets that preserve architecture — together they define the lab's biointegrated-electrophysiology toolkit for standardized organoid/assembloid functional assays.

## Open questions / limitations
- **Author-stated:** FEM optimization is computationally intensive (N² scaling, <100,000-triangle ceiling), constraining more complex geometries; fabrication/assembly requires advanced expertise; shell-theory optimization only valid for ultrathin (≪1% thickness/curvature) devices, not thick meshes.
- **Author-stated future work:** integrate pH, O₂, and neurotransmitter sensors or micro-LEDs for localized stimulation; design interconnected-basket geometries for linear/loop multi-region assembloids; extend to other systems (cardiac organoids, cortico–spinal–muscle assembloids).
- **Noted:** Recording is limited to the 32 central electrodes / ~1 mm core, so coverage is a small fraction of a mm-scale organoid; only the bottom of hCOs is live-imaged (limited confocal penetration). Disease and assembloid analyses rest on modest device numbers (2–4 organoids per condition). No oscillation/network-synchrony or E–I decomposition analyses beyond firing-rate metrics are reported here.
