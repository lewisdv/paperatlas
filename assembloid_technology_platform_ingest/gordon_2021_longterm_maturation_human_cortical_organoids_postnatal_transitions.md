# Long-term maturation of human cortical organoids matches key early postnatal transitions

## Abstract
Human stem-cell-derived models promise to accelerate understanding of brain disorders, but their utility is limited by uncertainty over whether they can mature beyond mid- to late-fetal stages. The authors leveraged a directed differentiation protocol (human cortical spheroids, hCS) to comprehensively assess in vitro maturation. Based on genome-wide analysis of the epigenetic clock and transcriptomics, plus RNA editing, they observe that 3D human cortical organoids reach postnatal stages between **250 and 300 days**, a timeline paralleling in vivo development. They demonstrate several known developmental milestones, including switches in the histone deacetylase (HDAC) complex and NMDA receptor subunits, confirmed at protein and physiological levels. This suggests that important components of an intrinsic in vivo developmental program persist in vitro. Neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative disease-risk genes are mapped onto in vitro expression trajectories, provided as a resource and webtool (Gene Expression in Cortical Organoids, GECO).

## Key findings
- **Convergent multi-omic timing of a fetal→postnatal switch at ~250–300 days (~8–10 months) in vitro.** Epigenetic clock, transition mapping of the transcriptome, RNA editing, and canonical isoform switches all independently converge on hCS acquiring a postnatal signature between day 250 and day 300, paralleling the in vivo timeline. Claimed as the first multi-level demonstration that an in vitro human neural model matures far enough to acquire postnatal features.
- **Epigenetic (DNA-methylation) clock tracks culture age.** DNAmAge (Horvath pan-tissue clock, Infinium MethylationEPIC array) is monotonically correlated with differentiation day: **r = +0.76, P = 1.57×10⁻¹⁰** (n = 50 samples, 6 hiPSC lines, 5 individuals). Clock cannot predict absolute culture age (not calibrated for the tissue/newer array) but shows continuous, progressive maturation. Implies hCS could model epigenetic aspects of aging.
- **Differentiation time is the dominant driver of transcriptomic variance.** Top five PCs (57.1% of variance) all associate with differentiation time; variance partitioning gives median variance explained by time = 29.2%, versus median <0.01% for individual and cell line. Samples cluster by differentiation day, not by batch/individual/line.
- **Transition mapping to BrainSpan pins the prenatal→postnatal boundary.** Before 250 days, hCS map to prenatal BrainSpan stages 3–7; by day 250 a postnatal signature emerges, becoming clear by day 300; after day 300, increasing overlap with postnatal stages 8–13. (BrainSpan stage 8 = birth to 6 months.)
- **In vivo–like gene-expression and network trajectories.** GSEA of DE genes across prenatal-equivalent days 25–200 (1,940 down, 2,122 up at FDR<0.05): embryonic proliferation down, neuronal/synaptic up. Postnatal-equivalent days 200–400 (992 down, 1,369 up): translation and cortical-neuron terms, plus LTP and neurotransmitter-metabolic terms up — matching known in vivo processes.
- **Cell-type marker sequence recapitulated.** Radial-glia markers peak earliest → neuronal markers → mature astrocyte markers peak latest (~350–400 days, when cultures resemble postnatal stages). In vivo WGCNA modules preserved: neuronal modules peak at prenatal-equivalent ~100–300 days; glial modules up early (day 25, radial-glia proliferation) then again at postnatal-equivalent ~400–600 days (astrogenesis).
- **RNA editing (ADAR-mediated) is partially preserved.** Two in vivo BrainSpan editing modules are moderately preserved in hCS (Zsummary BSeditM1 = 9.3, BSeditM2 = 5.3). hCSeditM2 correlates with editing regulators FXR1 (r = −0.32, FDR = 0.04) and ADAR (r = +0.60, FDR = 5.1×10⁻⁶); FXR1 inhibits editing via ADAR interaction, relevant to ASD. Editing sites lie significantly near FMRP/FXR1P eCLIP binding sites; FXR1P-targeted sites overlap between BrainSpan and hCS modules (BSeditM1 vs hCSeditM1 OR = 7.71, FDR = 6.18×10⁻⁵⁷). ADAR increases postnatally; ADARB1 up prenatally; FXR1 down prenatally — all mirrored in vitro.
- **HDAC complex isoform switch.** hCS show the canonical developmental HDAC switch: HDAC2 decreases while HDAC1 and HDAC11 increase (confirmed by IHC: HDAC2 higher in progenitors than neurons). Caveat: HDAC1 is downregulated postnatally in vivo but stays elevated in hCS — attributed to missing cell types or activity-dependent processes.
- **NMDA receptor subunit switch (GRIN2B→GRIN2A) at the fetal→postnatal boundary.** In vivo this switch occurs soon after birth (BrainSpan stage 8, 0–6 months). In hCS the RNA switch occurs at day 250–300, matching the transcriptomic transition; GRIN2D→GRIN2C also switches. Western blot (day 51–408, n = 13 from 2 lines): GRIN2A rises with time, GRIN2B peaks at 200–250 days then falls by 350–400 days — switch between 250 and 300 days, mirroring RNA.
- **Physiological confirmation of the NMDA switch.** Whole-cell voltage-clamp of NMDA responses in hCS slices: response magnitude increases over time (r = +0.63, P = 6.94×10⁻⁴); the fraction of NMDA current blocked by the GRIN2B-specific antagonist ifenprodil decreases with differentiation time (beta logit regression B = −0.003, P = 1.58×10⁻³), i.e., proportionally fewer GRIN2B-enriched receptors at later stages — a functional postnatal-like transition.
- **No progressive cellular stress; homeostatic metabolism.** ER-stress and glycolysis pathway genes are only slightly higher in vitro than in vivo and follow a flat trajectory for up to ~21 months, arguing against progressive stress/dysfunction; the same genes are robustly detectable in vivo (BrainSpan and a 40,000-cell fetal-cortex scRNA-seq dataset). Interpreted as a different-but-homeostatic metabolic state, contradicting prior organoid "cell-stress" claims.
- **Disease-risk genes fall into distinct developmental trajectory clusters, many peaking late (>250 days).** ASD and SCZ risk genes → 5 temporal clusters each; ID → 4 clusters. Three trajectories shared across disorders: (1) an early neuronal/synaptic peak at ~150 days (ASD-C3, ID-C4, SCZ-C2; e.g., MYT1L in ASD-C3); (2) a progenitor/chromatin-modification pattern high at neurogenesis (<150d) and gliogenesis (>300d) (ASD-C2, ID-C1); (3) an astrocyte-enriched late peak (>250 days; ASD-C5, ID-C3, SCZ-C1). Epilepsy: 62% of genes peak at day 150 (ion-channel/neuronal), but 20% peak >250 days.
- **Neurodegenerative genes require long-term culture.** 3/4 clusters in Alzheimer's (AD) and 3/4 in Parkinson's (PD) peak >250 days (predicted postnatal). AD-C1 contains familial AD genes APOE, APP, PSEN2 (amyloid-beta formation); PD Mendelian genes PRKN, UCHL1, SNCA, PARK7, PINK1, LRRK2 all peak >250 days. PSP/FTD clusters also peak late — emphasizing the need for prolonged maturation to model these.
- **Intrinsic developmental clock + limits from missing external cues.** Findings support an internal differentiation clock guiding corticogenesis (consistent with mouse intrinsic-program data). Activity-dependent features fail at late stages: activity markers NPAS4 and ARC only partially preserved; activity-dependent clusters (ASD-C3, SCZ-C2, epilepsy-C3) lose fidelity after ~400 days due to lack of extensive network activity. GABAergic (GAD1), OPC/oligo (PLP1), and microglial (AIF1, ITGAM) markers do not follow cortical in vivo trajectories or are undetectable — these lineages are absent/rare in hCS.

## Methods
- **Cell lines:** 6 hiPSC lines from 5 healthy individuals (4 male, 1 female); one line (H2096/H20961) derived by the Gilad Lab (U. Chicago). Stanford IRB approval, informed consent. Cultured on mouse embryonic fibroblast feeders; mycoplasma-free.
- **hCS protocol (Pașca-lab directed differentiation, matrix-free suspension; Paşca 2015 / Yoon 2019):** intact hiPSC colonies lifted with dispase to ultra-low-attachment dishes; dual-SMAD inhibition with dorsomorphin (5 µM) + SB-431542 (10 µM) plus ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 (10 µM); days 6–24 neurobasal-A + B27(−vit A) + EGF/FGF2 (20 ng/ml each); days 25–42 + BDNF/NT3 (20 ng/ml each); day 43 onward unsupplemented neural medium, changed every 4 days.
- **Culture duration & sampling:** hCS cultured up to **694 days** in vitro across lines (per-line maxima: 0307-1 = 280d; 1205-4 = 587d; 2242-1 = 652d; 8858-1 = 694d; 8858-3 = 652d; H2096 = 371d). Samples collected opportunistically at **13 timepoints** (grouped: 25, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400, 450, 500, 600 days). Note protocol is stated to support >20 months (~21 months) of culture (neurogenesis→astrogenesis).
- **Bulk RNA-seq:** 62 samples (4 individuals, 5 lines). TruSeq stranded RiboZero Gold, 100-bp PE, HiSeq 4000; STAR v2.5.2b to hg38/Gencode v25; RSEM v1.3.0. Low-expression filter; 2 outliers (both >600d) removed via network connectivity (Z<−3). CQN normalization; Combat batch correction; ancestry (MDS on SNPs vs HapMap3.3) and Picard seqPC1–5 covariates regressed out. loess trajectory fits.
- **DNA methylation:** 50 samples (5 individuals, 6 lines). Infinium MethylationEPIC BeadChip; Noob normalization; Horvath pan-tissue epigenetic clock (also in vitro clock evaluated; results similar, pan-tissue reported).
- **Transition mapping:** rank–rank hypergeometric test (step 200 genes) comparing hCS vs BrainSpan; TMM normalization (edgeR); fold changes vs day-25 baseline in vitro / stage-2 (8–10 PCW) baseline in vivo via limma-voom, blocked by individual.
- **WGCNA:** in vitro network built (soft power 12, min module size 100), overlapped with published in vivo modules (Stein et al.) via Fisher's exact test.
- **RNA editing:** editing sites/modules identified in hCS and BrainSpan; integrated with FMRP/FXR1P eCLIP from adult human brain; permutation-based proximity P values.
- **Isoform-switch validation:** Western blot for GRIN2A/GRIN2B/synapsin-1 (β-actin normalization), day 51–408, n = 13 from 2 lines. Whole-cell voltage-clamp NMDA recordings in 200-µm hCS slices (AAV-hSyn1::GFP labeled), ifenprodil block quantified.
- **IHC / human tissue comparators:** cryosections stained for BRN2/POU3F2, CTIP2/BCL11B, SATB2, GFAP, SOX9, AQP4, MAP2, FXR1, HDAC2 at days 61–552. Primary human forebrain tissue at PCW21 used as comparator.
- **In vivo reference:** BrainSpan bulk RNA-seq (196 samples, 24 individuals: 9 female, 15 male; RIN≥8 QC) plus a 40,000-cell fetal-cortex scRNA-seq dataset.
- **Resource:** GECO webtool (Gene Expression in Cortical Organoids) at labs.dgsom.ucla.edu/geschwind — concurrent hCS + BrainSpan gene-trajectory browsing.

## Relevance to the brain-organoid ASD review
- **Core FIDELITY / maturation-window evidence.** Provides the quantitative anchor that unguided/directed 3D cortical organoids reach only *early postnatal* (birth–6 months equivalent, BrainSpan stage 8) states, and only after **250–300 days** of culture — with full astrocyte maturation and disease-gene expression peaking even later (>350–400 days). Directly supports the manuscript's fidelity rubric: which developmental windows are modelable, and how long that takes.
- **Disease phenotypes gated by maturation.** ASD/ID/SCZ risk genes partition into distinct temporal clusters; some (early synaptic, e.g., MYT1L) are testable in young hCS (~150 days), but astrocyte-enriched and neurodegeneration-related clusters demand cultures >250 days. Concrete guidance (via GECO) for choosing timepoints per disease gene — useful for the review's discussion of what an organoid must mature to before a phenotype is interrogable.
- **RNA editing / FMRP–FXR1 axis ties organoid fidelity to ASD biology.** Preservation of ADAR-driven editing modules and FMRP/FXR1P-proximal editing sites links long-term hCS to an ASD-implicated RNA-processing mechanism — but preservation is partial at late stages, flagging a fidelity gap for activity-dependent editing (e.g., GRIA2/GluA2).
- **Faster alternative = transplantation.** The paper's central limitation — postnatal-like states need very long, variable culture — is the motivation for [Revah 2022](revah_2022_maturation_circuit_integration_transplanted_human_cortical_organoids.md), where transplanting hCS into neonatal rat cortex accelerates neuronal maturation and adds sensory input/activity. Pair these: Gordon 2021 = maturation ceiling in vitro; Revah 2022 = in vivo transplantation as the accelerant that also supplies the missing physiological input.
- **Missing niche cells bound organoid fidelity.** Absence/rarity of GABAergic interneurons, microglia, OPCs/oligodendrocytes, and vasculature limits maturation of E/I balance and activity-dependent programs — the same rationale for assembloids (interneuron integration) and for the review's argument that single-region organoids under-model interneuron- and microglia-dependent ASD phenotypes.
- **Intrinsic clock framing.** Supports the theme that human corticogenesis timing is substantially cell-intrinsic (protracted "neoteny"), reinforcing why organoids are a valid but slow window on human-specific developmental tempo.

## Open questions / limitations
- Author-stated: culturing hCS this long is non-trivial and low-throughput; samples collected opportunistically, so 3 of 6 lines lack full-timecourse coverage. Variability of differentiation increases after **400 days** (line-to-line Spearman correlation drops from 0.96 at day 25 to 0.92 at day 600); reducing this late variability is essential for late-stage disease modeling.
- Epigenetic clocks cannot predict absolute culture age (uncalibrated for tissue/EPIC array; poor in brain), so DNAmAge is used qualitatively (monotonic trend), not as an absolute age readout.
- Activity-dependent features underperform: activity markers (NPAS4, ARC) only partially preserved; activity-dependent gene clusters lose fidelity >400 days due to lack of network activity; RNA-editing preservation incomplete (e.g., ADARB2 far less dynamic; FXR1/FMR1 late trajectories diverge from in vivo).
- Cell-type gaps: GABAergic neurons, microglia (mesoderm-derived), OPCs/oligodendrocytes, and endothelial/vasculature are absent or rare — proposed to be addressed via assembloids and co-culture (interneuron, microglia-like, oligodendrocyte, endothelial integration).
- Alternative splicing not resolvable here (needs deeper, larger in vivo + in vitro sample sets).
- Some late-stage expression changes reflect shifting cell proportions (e.g., rising astrocyte fraction) rather than per-cell changes; HDAC1 stays elevated in hCS vs its postnatal in vivo downregulation (attributed to missing cells / activity).
- Trajectories are protocol- and lineage-specific: GECO reflects this directed hCS protocol; other protocols and brain regions may mature on different timelines.
- Overarching need: methods to *accelerate* maturation to make late-fetal/postnatal and neurodegenerative modeling efficient (transplantation being one such route).
