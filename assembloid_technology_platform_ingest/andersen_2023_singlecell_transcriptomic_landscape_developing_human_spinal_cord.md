## Abstract
Understanding spinal cord assembly is essential to elucidate how motor behavior is controlled and how disorders arise. The human spinal cord is exquisitely organized, and this complex organization contributes to the diversity and intricacy of motor behavior and sensory processing. But how this complexity arises at the cellular level in the human spinal cord remains unknown. Here the authors transcriptomically profiled the midgestation human spinal cord with single-cell resolution and discovered remarkable heterogeneity across and within cell types. Glia displayed diversity related to positional identity along the dorso-ventral and rostro-caudal axes, while astrocytes with specialized transcriptional programs mapped into white and gray matter subtypes. Motor neurons clustered at this stage into groups suggestive of alpha and gamma neurons. They also integrated their data with multiple existing datasets of the developing human spinal cord spanning 22 weeks of gestation to investigate cell diversity over time. Together with mapping of disease-related genes, this transcriptomic mapping of the developing human spinal cord opens new avenues for interrogating the cellular basis of motor control in humans and guides human stem cell-based models of disease.

## Key findings
- **Primary in-vivo atlas of midgestation human spinal cord.** 10x Chromium 3′ v3 scRNA-seq + snRNA-seq on **4 samples at GW17–GW18**; after QC/doublet removal, **112,554 single cells + 34,884 single nuclei** retained (from 138,159 cells and 44,131 nuclei called by Cell Ranger; 25,605 cells and 9,247 nuclei removed as doublets/low-quality). GW17 and GW18 highly correlated, analyzed jointly. To capture rare motor neurons (MNs), **Thy1 (CD90) immunopanning** (cells) and **NeuN sorting** (nuclei) were used to enrich neurons.
- **Cell-type taxonomy (integrated UMAP → 53 clusters, manually merged).** Astrocytes and cycling astrocytes; astroglia-lineage midline/VZ cells (floor plate FP, roof plate RP, midplate MP); oligodendrocyte lineage (OPC/oligo); a bridging **multipotent glial progenitor cell (mGPC)** population linking the astroglia and OPC lineages; neurons (enriched in nuclei); and vascular/immune cells: endothelial cells (EC), pericytes, vascular leptomeningeal cells (VLMC), Schwann cells, microglia/monocytes (MG/immune), RBC.
- **OPC/oligo lineage trajectory (n = 41,977 cells + 5,371 nuclei).** RNA velocity (scVelo dynamical mode) + diffusion pseudotime ordered the full oligodendrocyte lineage: **OPC → COP (committed oligo precursor) → NFOL (newly formed) → MFOL (myelin-forming) → MOL (mature oligo)**, plus cycling OPCs and OPC-like mGPCs. 500 most-variable genes clustered (k-means, 10 groups) across **419 pseudobulk bins** (100 cells/bin); GO shifted early cell-division → axon guidance/migration (NFOL/MFOL) → myelination (MFOL/MOL). Example dynamic gene: GPC3 (high velocity in COP, high expression in NFOL, downregulated in MFOL).
- **Astrocyte diversity along two orthogonal axes (n = 47,677 cells + 11,232 nuclei; 9 astrocyte clusters).** **Axis 1 = dorso-ventral position**: dorsal markers PAX3, ZIC1; ventral markers EPHA5, SEMA3A; a ventral **V1 astrocyte** subset (RELN+, SLIT1−). Validated by PAX3/NKX6-1 IHC. Positional homeodomain/forkhead TFs expressed subtype-specifically (PAX7, IRX2, DBX2, FOXP2, NKX6-1, NKX2-2, NKX6-2).
- **Axis 2 = white- vs gray-matter astrocytes.** **Fibrous (white matter)**: CRYAB, ID3, high GFAP; validated CRYAB in myelinated (SMI-312+) white matter. **Protoplasmic (gray matter)**: SLC7A10, KCNJ16 (Kir5.1), high SLC1A2 (EAAT2) and GLUL; validated GLUL/SLC1A2 in gray matter by IHC and fluorescence-intensity quantification. Novel marker: **CD38 specific to ventral fibrous astrocytes**.
- **Positional/functional coupling in astrocytes.** Subtypes differentially express Na⁺/K⁺-ATPases (fibrous ATP1A2/ATP1B2/ATP1B1/ATP1A1; protoplasmic higher ATP1A2/ATP1B2), glutamate receptors (fibrous GRM7/GRM8; protoplasmic GRM3/GRM5), neurotransmitter transporters (protoplasmic higher SLC1A2/SLC7A10/SLC6A9 → clearance role), and node-of-Ranvier/paranodal genes in fibrous (EZR, RDX, TNC, TNR). NATMI cell–cell interaction analysis: **ventral fibrous astrocytes ↔ MOL** showed the highest interaction specificity.
- **Midline glia / VZ progenitors (VZ subcluster n = 2,964 cells + 1,890 nuclei).** RP: GDF7, GDF10, ZIC1, MSX1; FP: SHH, FOXA2, PAX7. Discovered **PPP1R17+ FP/RP midline cells** (PPP1R17 is a cortical IPC marker with human-specific gene regulation) with radial glia-like morphology spanning VZ→pia, expressing midline-crossing guidance cues (NTN1/Netrin, SLITs, semaphorins, EFNB3); **minimal Ppp1r17 in mouse** VZ → candidate human-specific feature. Patterning at this stage differs from earlier embryonic: NKX6-1 not restricted ventrally (spans MP/FP); OLIG2 absent from VZ despite ongoing oligodendrogenesis.
- **mGPC multipotency.** EGFR⁺/OLIG2⁺/ASCL1⁺ progenitors bridge astroglia and OPC/oligo clusters; ~half express astroglia markers (SOX9, AQP4), half OPC markers (SOX10, PDGFRA). IHC localizes them to intermediate gray matter adjacent to VZ (SOX9+ or NKX2-2+). Analogous to a cortical EGFR+ mGPC population and to dual-lineage repair progenitors.
- **Neuronal diversity (neuron subcluster n = 8,669 cells + 13,816 nuclei).** Neurotransmitter classes: glutamatergic (SLC17A6/VGLUT2), GABAergic + GABA/glycinergic (GAD1, GAD2, SLC32A1/VGAT, SLC6A5/GLYT2), cholinergic (SLC5A7, SLC18A3, CHAT). Label transfer to the harmonized mouse spinal atlas (Russ et al. 2021) recovered excitatory/inhibitory/cholinergic and dorsal/ventral/intermediate types — but **only 22% of top-50 markers were shared human↔mouse** (rising to **~55%** when using all human markers at adj. P < 0.01), consistent with human–mouse divergence elsewhere in CNS.
- **Motor-neuron subtypes: early α/γ diversification (MN subcluster n = 78 cells + 277 nuclei).** Two cholinergic clusters: visceral MNs (NOS1) and skeletal MNs (RET, GFRA1). Label transfer from adult mouse MNs (Blum et al. 2021) split skeletal MNs into **α and γ**: **αMN = CHODL, SV2B, VIPR2**; **γMN = ESRRB, WLS, NDNF**. RNAScope: CHODL and NDNF mark distinct, non-overlapping CHAT+ ventral-horn MNs at GW18–19; NDNF appears **human-enriched in developing γMNs** (Chodl/Ndnf not separated in mouse P0/P25). Human GW17–18 αMNs uniquely express type-II cadherins (CDH7/9/10/20, motor-pool segregation) vs adult mouse.
- **Disease-gene mapping onto the atlas.** Leukodystrophy/myelin genes split across oligo/Schwann (PLP1, FA2H, GJC2), astrocytes (GJA1, GFAP, EDNRB), microglia (CSF1R, TREM2). CMT genes across OPC/oligo/Schwann (MPZ, PMP22), astrocytes (FGD4), microglia (EGR2), ubiquitous (KIF1B, SBF2). **ALS genes largely non-MN/ubiquitous** (SOD1 ubiquitous; MOBP in OPC/oligo; SQSTM1/GRN in VLMC/pericytes/microglia; only NEFH/PRPH MN-specific) → supports non-cell-autonomous toxicity.
- **Cross-dataset temporal integration spanning 22 weeks of gestation.** Merged this study with Rayon et al. 2021 and Zhang et al. 2021 → **35 samples, 950,215 cells+nuclei after QC** (783,408 cells + 166,807 nuclei), ~GW4–GW25 (CS12/GW4 to GW25). Temporal trends: myelinating oligos enriched late; VZ progenitors (cycling/FP/RP/MP) decline over time; **protoplasmic astrocytes precede fibrous** (protoplasmic GLUL present at GW11, fibrous CRYAB absent at GW11); α/γ MN signatures already separable as early as **GW8–10** (far earlier than mouse, where earliest γMN marker is E17.5).
- **Rostro-caudal (positional) glial diversity.** No clear regional bias in OPC/oligo; but **thoracic** segments had fewer protoplasmic astrocytes (ventral proto-2 cluster only 7.4% thoracic vs 37.9% average elsewhere), across all timepoints — a dorso-ventral × rostro-caudal positional code for glia.

## Methods
- **Tissue:** de-identified human spinal cord from elective pregnancy terminations (Stanford IRB/Research Compliance approved); processed within 3 h. Primary scRNA/snRNA-seq samples at **GW17 and GW18** (n = 4); additional IHC/RNAScope validation at GW11, GW18, GW19; mouse spinal cord at P0 (n = 4) and P25 (n = 3).
- **Capture:** 10x Chromium Single Cell 3′ v3; targets 10,000 cells / 8–10,000 nuclei / 4,000 NeuN-sorted nuclei. Neuron enrichment via **Thy1/CD90 immunopanning** (cells) and **NeuN FACS** (nuclei). Nuclei isolated by detergent-mechanical lysis + sucrose gradient.
- **Sequencing/alignment:** NovaSeq S4, 150×2; Cell Ranger 5.0.0 aligned to GRCh38 (2020-A) with `--include-introns`; spliced/unspliced counts via velocyto 0.17.17.
- **Analysis:** Seurat (v3.9.9.9024 / v4 beta), R 3.6.1. QC filters: remove cells/nuclei with <1,000 features or >30% mitochondrial reads; iterative clustering + marker-based doublet removal (co-expression of ≥2 lineage markers → doublet). CCA integration (2,000 features, 60 dims) → 53 clusters → manual annotation. Subclustering of OPC/oligo, astroglia, VZ (15 dims/1,000 features), neurons, and MNs (8 dims/750 features). Cell-type markers: SOX10/OLIG2/PDGFRA/MOG/MBP (OPC/oligo), AQP4/GFAP/FGFR3/SOX9 (astroglia), STMN2/MYT1L (neurons), AIF1 (microglia), TOP2A (cycling), PECAM1 (EC), NGFR (Schwann), COL1A1 (VLMC).
- **Trajectory:** scVelo 0.2.3 dynamical mode; diffusion pseudotime; 419 pseudobulk bins of 100 cells; k-means (10 groups) on 500 variable genes.
- **Cross-species/label transfer:** CCA projection (25 dims) onto mouse and human references (Russ 2021, Blum 2021, Delile 2019, Rayon 2021, Zhang 2021, and others via GEO); mouse→human gene symbol homolog conversion.
- **Cell–cell interaction:** NATMI (specificity cutoff 0.05, detection 0.1). DE by Wilcoxon rank-sum (`FindMarkers`/`FindAllMarkers`, min.pct 0.25, adj. P < 0.01, ranked by logFC); GO via fgsea/fora.
- **Integration:** combined with Rayon et al. 2021 + Zhang et al. 2021 (300,000 cells randomly subsampled from Zhang); 3,000 variable features; nearest-neighbor (FNN `get.knnx`) label propagation from this study's annotations. Age bins: embryonic GW4–9, early fetal 1 GW10–11, early fetal 2 GW12–14, early mid-fetal 1 GW15–17, early mid-fetal 2 GW18–19, late mid-fetal GW20–25.
- **Validation:** IHC and RNAScope Multiplex Fluorescent V2 (CHAT, CHODL, NDNF probes; human and mouse); confocal imaging.

## Relevance to the brain-organoid ASD review
- **Primary in-vivo fetal reference atlas — exactly the benchmark a FIDELITY rubric requires.** This is a ground-truth, quantitative human fetal spinal cord census (112,554 cells + 34,884 nuclei at GW17–18; integrated 950,215 cells over GW4–25). It is the type of primary tissue dataset against which hiPSC-derived neural models must be scored, complementing cortical fetal references ([Nowakowski 2017](nowakowski_2017_spatiotemporal_gene_expression_trajectories_reveal_developmental_hierarchies_human.md), [Trevino 2021](trevino_2021_chromatin_gene_regulatory_dynamics_developing_human_cerebral.md)) and cortical-organoid-vs-fetal benchmarks ([Cheroni 2022](cheroni_2022_benchmarking_brain_organoid_recapitulation_fetal_corticogenesis.md), [Kanton 2019](kanton_2019_organoid_single-cell_genomic_atlas.md)).
- **Extends the DBTL / organoid-fidelity logic beyond cortex to spinal cord.** From the Pașca lab (senior author; same group behind cortical spheroids [Pașca 2015](paca_2015_functional_cortical_neurons_astrocytes_human_pluripotent_stem.md) and assembloids [Birey 2017](birey_2017_assembly_functionally_integrated_human_forebrain_spheroids.md), [Miura 2022](miura_2022_engineering_brain_assembloids_to_interrogate.md)); the paper explicitly states its goal is to "guide human stem cell-based models of disease" and to "improve organoid and assembloid models." It supplies the reference atlas for spinal-cord organoids/assembloids used to model ALS, spinal muscular atrophy, and leukodystrophies.
- **Dorso-ventral (and rostro-caudal) patterning benchmark for morphogen-guided organoids.** The DV positional code (dorsal PAX3/ZIC1 vs ventral EPHA5/SEMA3A/NKX6-1; FP SHH/FOXA2; RP GDF7/GDF10) and its coupling to glial/astrocyte identity is the fidelity yardstick for SHH/RA/BMP-patterned protocols — directly relevant to multiplexed morphogen-screening organoid work ([Amin 2024 morphogen](amin_2024_generating_human_neural_diversity_multiplexed_morphogen_screen_organoids.md); note Neal D. Amin is a co-author here).
- **Cell-type marker panel + temporal trajectories for scoring.** Provides validated markers (α: CHODL/SV2B/VIPR2; γ: NDNF/ESRRB/WLS; fibrous: CRYAB; protoplasmic: SLC7A10/GLUL; FP/RP: PPP1R17) and developmental timing (protoplasmic-before-fibrous astrocytes; α/γ MN split by GW8–10) that an organoid can be benchmarked against for cell-type identity and maturation stage.
- **Human-specific developmental features caution against uncritical mouse benchmarking.** Only 22% top-marker overlap human↔mouse, human-enriched NDNF+ γMNs, PPP1R17+ midline glia, and much earlier α/γ diversification than rodent — reinforcing the review's argument that human-derived models (and human fetal references), not rodent proxies, are essential for ASD/neurodevelopmental fidelity claims.
- **Disease-gene cell-type mapping supports non-cell-autonomous disease modeling.** ALS/CMT/leukodystrophy risk genes are broadly distributed across glia and vascular cells, not just neurons — a rationale for multi-lineage organoid/assembloid models over pure-neuron cultures.

## Open questions / limitations
- Single-cell vs single-nucleus artifacts: cells showed dissociation-related immediate-early ("active"/FOS/EGR1/ARC) and stress signatures largely absent from nuclei; some clusters (e.g., "active" astrocytes) are likely dissociation artifacts rather than in-vivo states.
- Primary sampling is narrow in age (**only GW17–18**); relationships between cell types (e.g., whether protoplasmic and fibrous astrocytes share a common progenitor; whether mGPCs derive from VZ/ependymal cells) require earlier timepoints and lineage tracing not done here.
- Human↔mouse comparisons are confounded by **mismatched developmental ages** (human GW17–18 vs adult mouse for MN α/γ mapping), so some divergence may reflect age, not species.
- Functional coupling to positional identity in astrocytes is inferred from gene expression only — not functionally demonstrated.
- α/γ MN and visceral-MN separation was not resolved in early mouse (E9.5–13.5) or early human embryonic (Rayon) datasets using these markers, leaving the exact onset of human diversification approximate ("relatively early fetal").
- Whether PPP1R17 plays a genuinely human-specific role in the spinal cord is stated as unresolved.
- GW22–24 Zhang et al. samples contributed few neurons (myelinated-tissue dissociation difficulty), so the integrated neuronal subcluster is dominated by GW4–21 — late-fetal neuronal dynamics are under-sampled.
- NeuN-sorted nuclei had low "fraction of reads in cells" (possible ambient RNA contamination).
- **This is a primary fetal tissue atlas — it does not itself derive or directly benchmark spinal-cord organoids/assembloids**; the organoid-fidelity relevance is as a reference resource, not an in-vitro comparison performed in the paper.
