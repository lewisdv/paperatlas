# Stretchable mesh microelectronics for the biointegration and stimulation of human neural organoids

## Abstract
3D culture now yields organoids recapitulating cellular and physiological features of human nervous-system domains, but long-term microelectrode interfaces with *free-floating* organoids remain limited. The authors report a stretchable, soft mesh electrode system that forms an intimate in vitro electrical interface with human neurons in 3D organoids. The mesh uses PEDOT:PSS-based electrically conductive hydrogel electrode arrays with poly(styrene-ethylene-butylene-styrene) (SEBS) as substrate and encapsulation. The mesh maintains stable electrochemical impedance in buffer under 50% compressive and 50% tensile strain. PSC-derived human cortical organoids (hCO) were cultured on the mesh for >3 months and readily integrated with it. Using simultaneous stimulation + calcium imaging, electrical stimulation through the mesh elicited intensity-dependent calcium signals comparable to stimulation from a bipolar stereotrode. Proposed as a tool for monitoring and modulating electrical activity of in vitro models of neuropsychiatric disease.

## Key findings
- **Intrinsically stretchable mesh chemistry.** Both electrodes and interconnects are PEDOT:PSS conductive-polymer hydrogel (physically crosslinked via addition/removal of an ionic-liquid additive); top/bottom insulation is SEBS elastomer. This contrasts with prior organoid meshes built from *non-stretchable* parylene-C (Park et al. 2021) or SU-8 (Le Floch et al. 2021), which are flexible but high-modulus and cannot adapt to a growing organoid.
- **Ultra-thin, geometrically stretchable design.** Rectangular mesh with diagonal cross-connections, overall thickness **5 µm** (height mapping); a **40 nm Au** layer sits atop the PEDOT:PSS interconnect to lower resistance of the high-aspect-ratio lines. Each electrode = exposed PEDOT:PSS disk of **50 µm diameter**. Released from a Si wafer via a water-soluble **dextran (5 wt%)** sacrificial layer (aqueous lift-off).
- **Electrode count / channels.** Devices are **16-electrode / 16-channel** meshes (interfaced via a 16-channel Intan RHS Stim/Recording headstage, M4016). Characterization pooled **118 electrodes across 8 meshes from 4 separate batches**.
- **Low impedance.** Sample 16-electrode mesh averaged **55.8 ± 19.7 kΩ** at 1 kHz; excluding damaged electrodes (>1 MΩ), average **20.1 ± 36.1 kΩ at 1 kHz** across the 118-electrode pool. Impedance declines with frequency (measured 20 Hz–5 kHz). One outlier electrode 7.6 MΩ; two shorted/low channels at 0.81 and 7.8 kΩ.
- **Low noise.** Working-electrode RMS noise in PBS = **5.0 ± 0.7 µV**.
- **Strain-invariant electrical performance.** Impedance (20–5000 Hz) did **not** significantly change under 50% strain in either compression or stretch (two-way ANOVA, p = 0.921 for strain). Bulk PEDOT:PSS retains conductivity after 1000 cycles to 100% strain (prior work, Liu et al. 2019). Mechanically very soft: FEA (Mooney-Rivlin hyperelastic, FEniCS) shows peak stress concentrated at mesh crossings (~**40 kPa**); <0.2 g weight elongates the mesh 50%.
- **High stimulation current density.** Under bipolar pulsed voltage (25 mV–1.5 V, 50 Hz), the mesh delivers a max current of **131 µA** within the water-splitting window (1.23 V) = current density **1.67 A/cm²** — attributed to nanoporous, electrolyte-filled PEDOT:PSS giving high electrochemical surface area.
- **Long-term biointegration (>3 months).** hCOs seeded on the mesh at differentiation day 10 grew *into and around* the mesh; the organoid-mesh hybrid grows to several millimeters. Rhodamine-doped SEBS + TUBA1B-mEGFP (GFP-α-tubulin) hCOs showed eGFP-positive tissue encapsulating the red mesh lines by **day 50** after plating.
- **Deep integration confirmed by clearing.** A dye-free mesh seeded at day 32 and fixed at day 146 (**114 days on mesh**), stained for MAP2 (neuronal dendrites) + GFAP (glia) and CUBIC-cleared, showed the hCO well-integrated into the mesh in 3D. **No increased GFAP near the mesh** (no obvious glial/foreign-body reaction) and little MAP2 difference near vs. far.
- **No cytotoxic edge effect (quantitative IHC).** Cryosections at day 100 (DAPI, MAP2, TUJ1): fluorescence within **20 µm of mesh-fragment edges** vs. rest of organoid showed **no significant difference** for DAPI, MAP2, or TUJ1 across 10 fields of view / 4 sections (Wilcoxon matched-pairs: TUJ1 p = 0.8457, MAP2 p = 0.2754).
- **Electrode embedding validated via pH.** Open-circuit-potential-derived local pH inside the integrated organoid ranged **6.4–7.2, mean 6.7 ± 0.4** — slightly acidic, consistent with electrodes sitting inside metabolically active tissue.
- **Stimulation elicits intensity-dependent Ca²⁺ responses (mesh).** GCaMP7f-AAV hCO cultured on mesh 80 days (total diff. day 90), stimulated through all 16 channels (100 pulses, 100 Hz): **100 µA** drove larger, sustained responses reaching 3 of 4 spatial regions; **20 µA** was smaller and affected only 1 region. Pulse-number titration: **20 or 10 pulses** (20 µA) elicited activity, **5 pulses insufficient**. Spontaneous bursts recurred minutes post-stimulation. Each stimulation biphasic, cathodic-first, 200 µs/phase.
- **Stereotrode benchmark (thresholds).** Bipolar stereotrode in a day-129 GCaMP6f hCO defined minimal parameters: 100 pulses @ 250 Hz — **50 µA** evoked signal at proximal + distal ROIs, **20/10 µA** proximal only, **5 µA** none; at 20 µA, **50 Hz** reached the distal ROI while 250 Hz did not; as few as **5 pulses** (20 µA, 50 Hz) evoked a proximal response. Mesh results were consistent with this benchmark, i.e., the mesh performs comparably to a traditional bipolar electrode.
- **Recording is the acknowledged gap.** Reliable electrical *recording* through these meshes "requires additional work"; the demonstrated functional readout is stimulation-evoked calcium imaging, not electrophysiological recording.

## Methods
- **Mesh fabrication.** PEDOT:PSS (Clevios PH1000, 0.172 g in 15 mL) + ~50 wt% ionic liquid (4-(3-butyl-1-imidazolio)-1-butanesulfonic acid triflate), 0.45 µm filtered. Dextran (5 wt%) spin-coated on Si as sacrificial layer. SEBS (20% in toluene) spin-coated 4000 rpm/1 min, annealed 100 °C/10 min; O₂-plasma treated (150 W/1 min). PEDOT:PSS/IL drop- or spin-cast (2000 rpm/1 min), baked 110 °C/20 min. 40 nm Au e-beam/thermal evaporated. Patterned by photolithography (S1805 resist, Quintel Q4000 aligner) using **metal hard masks (Au, then 60 nm Al)** to protect polymer from resist swelling; Au patterned by Ar ion milling; PEDOT:PSS/SEBS etched by ICP-RIE (PlasmaTherm) / O₂ plasma; top Al mask removed with FeCl₃. Contacts: anisotropic conductive tape (3M 9703, 1 mm pitch) + FFC jumper cables. Device released by soaking in water ≥1 day (dextran dissolution), then O₂-plasma (150 W/15 s) and geltrex-coated overnight at 37 °C.
- **Mechanical simulation.** SEBS treated as hyperelastic; Mooney-Rivlin energy functional; FEniCS FEA on the mesh CAD envelope (gold neglected). Dirichlet BCs, 50% biaxial elongation. Code: github.com/bcuilab/stretching_simulation.
- **Electrical characterization.** Intan RHS Stim/Recording System via NHD-FFC16-1 breakout → 16-pin adapter (Intan B7600) → M4016 headstage. Impedance/noise/stimulation via Intan RHS software; strain impedance measured by pulling floating mesh with a fine tweezer. Also EIS with Biologic VSP-300 potentiostat (PEIS, 1 Hz–1 MHz, 10 mV, Ag/AgCl ref, in PBS). Equivalent circuit modeled as a Randles circuit.
- **hCO generation.** Cortical organoids ("cortical spheroids") per Pașca lab method (Paşca et al. 2015): hiPSC on DR4 feeders → dispase lift → ultra-low-attachment plates; dual-SMAD (dorsomorphin 5 µM + SB-431542 10 µM) to day 5, Y-27632 first 24 h; day 6 switch to Neurobasal-A/B-27(-vitA)/GlutaMax; FGF2 20 ng/mL + EGF 20 ng/mL through day ~19–25; then BDNF 20 ng/mL + NT3 20 ng/mL from day 25; growth factors removed day 43+.
- **hCO integration / culture rig.** Custom **hinged-lid dish** from a 60 mm ultra-low-attachment plate: laser-cut (Epilog Fusion M2) slot for the FFC + a full cut splitting the lid, re-joined with autoclave tape acting as a hinge — lets top-side electrical contacts stay above media and allows media changes without disturbing the mesh/FFC. Meshes geltrex-coated (1:100 in PBS, 24 h, which also dissolves dextran). **3–5 hCOs at day 10** corralled over the geltrex-coated mesh by an autoclaved Teflon ring (55D durometer); organoids attach over ~24–48 h, then the ring is removed and the mesh-organoid assembly floats freely.
- **Imaging / clearing.** CUBIC protocol (Tainaka 2018): 4% PFA fix; CUBIC-L 48 h/37 °C; MAP2 + GFAP primaries (48 h/37 °C), secondaries Alexa GuineaPig-647 / Rabbit-568 (1:500); CUBIC-R+ clearing; Leica SP8 confocal. Cryosections: 30% sucrose, OCT, −80 °C, 50 µm sections; DAPI/MAP2 (Synaptic Systems 188004)/TUJ1 (Biolegend 801202).
- **pH measurement.** Open-circuit potential (Palmsens potentiostat) between mesh and reference electrode, calibrated against standard pH solutions.
- **Stimulation setup.** Intan RHS 16-channel headstage/system. GCaMP7f-AAV (pAAV.CAG.GCaMP6f.WPRE.SV40, Addgene) added to dish, media exchanged 24 h later, matured 10 or 14 days before imaging. Probe benchmark: bipolar Pt/Ir probe (Microprobes PI2ST30.1A10) on a Sutter MP-225 micromanipulator. Confocal (Leica SP8, OKO Lab stage-top incubator) trigger unit wired to Intan digital input for frame-locked stimulation (Leica LASX Live Data Mode). All stimulations biphasic, cathodic-first, 200 µs/phase.
- **Timepoints (summary).** Seeding at diff. day 10 (also day 32); live integration imaging day 50 on-mesh; CUBIC fix day 146 (114 days on-mesh); cryosection IHC day 100; stereotrode threshold experiments day 129; mesh stimulation at diff. day 90 (80 days on-mesh); culture demonstrated >3 months.

## Relevance to the brain-organoid ASD review
- **Directly addresses the "poorly standardized functional electrophysiology" gap.** The manuscript argues MEA/electrophysiology readouts of organoids are non-standardized and hard to deploy. This paper is an *enabling hardware platform* for repeated, same-organoid functional interrogation over months — a step toward standardized, longitudinal functional assays rather than terminal/acute recordings (slicing, patch clamp, planar-MEA plating).
- **Chronic, non-destructive, same-sample readouts.** Because the mesh integrates into the 3D structure and stretches as the organoid grows, the *same* organoid can be stimulated (and in principle recorded) across many timepoints — valuable for tracking developmental trajectories and drug/perturbation effects in patient-derived (e.g., ASD) lines, addressing the reproducibility/longitudinal-comparison problem central to the review.
- **Closed-loop modulation potential for disease models.** Authors explicitly frame the tool for "monitoring and modulating electrical activity of in vitro models of neuropsychiatric diseases" — relevant to functional-phenotype rescue/perturbation experiments in ASD-associated genotypes.
- **Quantitative biocompatibility evidence supports deployability.** Multiple orthogonal readouts (CUBIC clearing, cryosection IHC within 20 µm of mesh, no GFAP up-regulation) show the electrode does not distort tissue architecture or provoke gliosis — important when the readout must not confound the phenotype being measured.
- **Pașca-lab provenance ties it to the ASD-organoid ecosystem.** Same lab/organoid protocol as the cortical-organoid and assembloid work cited throughout the review (see Related), so the platform is directly compatible with the review's model systems.
- **Complement to kirigami-type organoid electronics.** This is a *stretchable-mesh* (whole-organoid, stimulation-validated) approach; the companion **Yang 2024 kirigami** electronics represent a distinct conformal-electrode strategy for electrophysiological *recording* from organoids/assembloids. Together they illustrate the emerging but still non-standardized landscape of organoid functional-interface hardware. NOTE: the Yang 2024 kirigami source page is **not yet ingested** in this collection (link pending — see Related).

## Open questions / limitations
- **Recording not yet achieved.** Authors state reliable electrical *recording* through the mesh "requires additional work"; only stimulation-evoked calcium imaging is demonstrated. Simultaneous record+stimulate (true closed-loop) is future work.
- **Scalability.** Authors note the system's scalability needs improvement (batch yield: ~2–3 of 16 electrodes per sample mesh were damaged/shorted; 118 "good" electrodes pooled from 8 meshes).
- **Calcium imaging as proxy.** Functional readout relies on GECIs (GCaMP6f/7f) + confocal imaging, not direct electrophysiology — lower temporal resolution than electrical recording and requires optical access.
- **Mechanistic uncertainty.** The impact of a mesh's modulus on interactions with a *growing* organoid is stated as unknown (motivation, not resolved); slightly acidic intra-organoid pH (mean 6.7) is inferred, not independently validated.
- **Sample sizes modest.** Integration/biocompatibility claims rest on small n (e.g., 10 FOV / 4 sections; single representative cleared and live-imaged organoids); stimulation demonstrated on individual organoids.
- **Not disease-specific here.** Demonstrated on wild-type reference hCO lines (TUBA1B-mEGFP; standard hiPSC); no ASD or other disease genotype tested in this study.
- **Future readouts proposed but unproven.** Authors suggest the electrodes could also monitor pH and neurotransmitter concentrations over time — not demonstrated.
