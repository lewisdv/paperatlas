## Abstract
Among primates, humans display a unique trajectory of development responsible for many species-specific traits, but inaccessibility of primary human and chimpanzee tissue has limited study of human evolution. Comparative in vitro approaches using primate iPS cells have begun to reveal cellular/molecular species differences, and brain organoids are a promising platform for primate neural development, although cross-species organoid comparisons are complicated by differences in developmental timing and variability of differentiation. Here the authors develop a platform that fuses human and chimpanzee iPS cells to generate a panel of **tetraploid hybrid stem cells**, and apply it to cortical development by differentiating them into neural organoids. Hybrid organoids provide a controlled system for disentangling **cis- and trans-acting gene-expression divergence** across cell types and developmental stages, revealing a **signature of selection on astrocyte-related genes**. They also identify upregulation of the human **somatostatin receptor 2 gene (SSTR2)**, which regulates neuronal calcium signalling and is associated with neuropsychiatric disorders, and reveal a **human-specific response to modulation of SSTR2 function** in cortical neurons, underscoring the platform's potential for elucidating the molecular basis of human evolution.

## Key findings
- **Core innovation — human×chimpanzee tetraploid hybrid iPS cells (hyiPS).** PEG (PEG 1500)-mediated fusion of fluorescently-labelled human + chimp iPS cells, then FACS for double-positive cells. The double-positive fused population was **4.16% of filtered cells** in the PEG group vs **1.26%** in the no-PEG control. Generated **5 hyiPS lines** from **2 parental pairs** (age- and sex-matched): **3 male (XY/XY, "Hy1" prefix)** and **2 female (XX/XX, "Hy2")** (Supplementary Table 1). Parental panel = **3 human (H20961, H21792, H20682) + 3 chimp (C3649, C3647, C3651)** lines from the Gilad lab (Gallego Romero/Ward eLife panel).
- **Why hybrids solve the cis/trans problem.** Within a single hybrid nucleus, both alleles share the identical **trans-regulatory environment** and all non-genetic/batch factors; therefore any **allele-specific expression (ASE)** must reflect **cis-acting divergence**. Comparing hybrid ASE to parental (between-line) divergence partitions expression differences into cis vs trans/non-genetic components.
- **Cis/trans decomposition works — worked example NRP2.** The ~**11.6-fold** higher NRP2 expression in human is decomposed into **3.5-fold cis** + **3.3-fold trans/non-genetic** divergence. Formula: cis% = |cis| / (|cis| + |trans/non-genetic|), where cis = hybrid log2(human/chimp) and trans/non-genetic = parental log2 − hybrid log2. Convention throughout: **positive log2FC = higher in human**.
- **Tetraploidy is benign; species identity is preserved.** hyiPS express pluripotency markers (SOX2, NANOG, MYCL, KLF4; PluriTest-confirmed). On total expression, hybrids sit **intermediate** between parents (PCA); on **allelic** expression, each hybrid subgenome clusters with its **species of origin**, showing each genome retains a species-specific expression profile independent of ploidy. Species-specific profiles are **maintained through neural differentiation**.
- **Karyotype stability.** Largely stable over time; recurrent **species-agnostic gain of chr20q** (a known iPS-culture artefact). During differentiation, only **3 chromosomal gains and no losses** observed. No species-biased X-inactivation; **mitochondrial mRNAs were of human origin**. (Aneuploid chromosomes excluded from analysis: chr20,X,Y for iPS; chr18,20,X,Y for spheroids.)
- **Hybrid cortical spheroids (hyCS).** Differentiated via the Pașca cortical-spheroid protocol; low initial success rescued by **embedding aggregates in a Matrigel droplet**, enabling 3 hyiPS lines to reach **200 days in vitro**. Produced VZ-like structures (SOX9/PAX6/TBR2+). scRNA-seq (SmartSeq2) recovered neural cell types — cycling progenitors (PAX6+, some HOPX+), astroglia (some mature AGT+), glutamatergic neurons (NEUROD6+/SLC17A7/VGLUT1+; deep TBR1+ and superficial SATB2+), and DLX5/DLX6+ GABAergic neurons — plus mesenchymal/epithelial cells attributed to Matrigel embedding. **706 cells** retained of 750; **368 neural cells** used for downstream analysis; **348** for Monocle2 trajectories (neurogenesis + astrogenesis branches).
- **Magnitude of cis divergence.** Bulk RNA-seq over up to 200 days identified **"thousands of genes with divergent cis-regulation."** Genome-wide, **~39% of expression differences between hCS and cCS are attributable to cis-regulatory divergence** (Ext. Data Fig. 9d,e). ASE trends were largely shared across time points, but **~40% of ASE effects were present only in hyCS and not in hyiPS** — i.e., emerge specifically during cortical differentiation.
- **Signature of selection on astrocyte genes.** A single WGCNA co-expression module (**MEsalmon**, astrocyte-related) was higher in hCS than cCS at late time points **and strongly human-biased in hyCS allelic expression** — a directional (lineage-specific) signal indicative of **selection** (upregulation in human or downregulation in chimp). Module genes: **AQP4, HEPACAM, CLU, HEPN1, S100B**; localized to astrocytes in scRNA-seq. Significantly human-biased ASE at every time point (Wilcoxon: P = 1.5×10⁻¹⁴ at day 50; 9.5×10⁻¹¹ at 100; 2.6×10⁻¹² at 150; 6.0×10⁻⁶ at 200). One of the most human-biased module genes is **PMP2** (peripheral myelin protein 2), previously proposed to underlie human/mouse cortical astrocyte morphological differences.
- **Heterochrony / neoteny signal.** CIBERSORT deconvolution of bulk samples showed a **slower progression from neurogenesis to astrogenesis in human (hCS)** vs chimp, consistent with the neotenic delay in human cortical development. Chimp spheroids (cCS) showed a species difference toward **mesenchymal cell-fate** specification; ASE analysis in hybrids showed **no allelic (cis) bias in cell fate**, indicating the parental cell-fate difference is trans/non-genetic, not cis.
- **Disease-associated cis divergence.** Among genes with significant ASE, **100 genes overlapped neurodevelopmental-disorder gene sets** (SFARI/AutDB, downloaded Dec 2019), e.g. **GRIN2A (chimp-biased)** and **SCN1A (human-biased)** — candidates linking human-specific cortical function and dysfunction.
- **Highlighted gene — SSTR2 (functional validation).** SSTR2 (a Gαi GPCR for somatostatin that suppresses voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels) showed **increasing human-biased ASE as spheroids mature**, expression restricted mainly to glutamatergic neurons. **Protein sequence is identical between human and chimp** (a purely regulatory difference). Human-biased bulk expression matched hybrid ASE; **SSTR2 protein higher in hCS- than cCS-derived neurons** (immunostaining, ****P<0.0001). In vivo, SSTR2 is more highly expressed in human adult cortex (esp. layers 5/6) than chimp/macaque, mirroring patterns altered in schizophrenia and Alzheimer's.
- **Human-specific calcium phenotype.** Pharmacological block of SSTR2 with antagonist **CYN-154806 (CYN, 0.5 µM)** revealed a species-specific functional consequence: (i) Fura-2 imaging (days 225–250, KCl depolarization) — CYN **increased** peak intracellular Ca²⁺ in **human** neurons (P = 4.14×10⁻¹⁴) but **not** chimp; (ii) GCaMP6s imaging in intact spheroids (days 130–150, glutamate uncaging) — CYN significantly **raised spontaneous Ca²⁺ transient frequency in human** (P = 0.00118) but not chimp neurons. Robust across multiple lines, assays, and time points.
- **Take-home.** Hybrid iPS/organoid fusion provides a controlled, batch-artefact-free platform that resolves gene-expression divergence into cis vs trans components across cell types and neurodevelopmental stages, yielding a **catalogue of hundreds–thousands of cis-divergent genes** (strongest at late-gestation-equivalent time points otherwise inaccessible in vivo) and demonstrating that human-specific *regulatory* changes translate into human-specific *cellular physiology*.

## Methods
- **iPS lines:** 3 human + 3 chimp episomally-reprogrammed fibroblast iPS lines from Y. Gilad lab (Univ. Chicago; chimp lines via Yerkes Primate Center). Feeder-free on vitronectin, Essential 8.
- **Hybrid generation:** label human iPS with CellTracker Deep Red, chimp with CellTracker Green CMFDA; co-culture (1M each per well); **PEG 1500, 2 min at 37 °C**; FACS sort Deep-Red⁺/Green⁺/DAPI⁻ onto MEF feeders; expand clonal lines (named by fusion pair 1/2 + colony no., e.g. Hy1-29). Karyotyping (GTW banding, Stanford Cytogenetics) + RNA-seq-based aneuploidy checks.
- **hCS/cCS/hyCS differentiation:** dorsomorphin + SB-431542 dual-SMAD neural induction; EGF/FGF2 (d6–24) then BDNF/NT3 (d25–42). **hyCS** required **Matrigel-droplet embedding** of d2–3 aggregates to rescue differentiation; up to **200 days** in vitro.
- **Bulk RNA-seq:** TruSeq Stranded mRNA, Illumina HiSeq (2×150 bp). hCS/cCS at **7 time points** (days 15, 25, 35, 50, 100, 150, 200); hyiPS + hyCS bulk at **4 time points** (days 50, 100, 150, 200).
- **Single-cell RNA-seq:** SmartSeq2 (FACS into 96-well plates), Illumina NovaSeq (2×100 bp, ~2M reads/cell); **2 hyiPS lines at day 150**; 750 cells → 706 pass QC (>100k transcripts, <7% mito). Seurat (top 1,500 variable genes, 14 PCs, UMAP); Monocle2 (DDRTree) trajectories.
- **Allele-specific mapping:** variants called from GRCh38 vs PanTro4/PanTro5 alignment (Ensembl), filtered to **~2,800,000 high-confidence phased loci**. STAR two-pass mapping to **both** genomes; **Hornet/WASP** to remove mapping-bias reads; **ASEr** to assign reads to species. Genes whose log2FC differed by >1 between the two genome mappings were removed (mapping-bias filter). Reads mapped to GRCh38 unless noted.
- **Stats/analysis:** DESeq2 (LRT) for differential expression (model ~Replicate+Species) and ASE (allelic counts; ~Replicate+CellLine+Species); **CIBERSORT** deconvolution (5 cell types); **WGCNA** (soft power 8, ≥30 genes/module) for co-expression modules; GOrilla for GO enrichment; SFARI overlap.
- **Functional assays:** Fura-2 ratiometric Ca²⁺ imaging (SYN1-eYFP-labelled dissociated neurons, days 225–250, 67 mM KCl depolarization); GCaMP6s imaging (AAV1 hSyn1::mRuby2-P2A-GCaMP6s, intact spheroids, days 130–150, MNI-caged-glutamate uncaging); SSTR2 antagonist **CYN-154806** 0.5 µM; two-tailed Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, Fisher's method to combine per-line P-values. Investigators blinded to species/line/treatment for GCaMP6s analysis.
- **Ethics note:** tetraploid cells **not approved for in vivo use or organism generation** (stated biologically unlikely); Stanford SCRO-approved; benchside bioethics consultation.

## Relevance to the brain-organoid ASD review
- **Direct methodological bridge for "human-specific features / heterochrony" and species-comparison themes.** Provides a controlled cis/trans decomposition that other organoid comparisons (Kanton 2019, Pollen 2019, Otani 2016) cannot — it separates *genetically encoded (cis) regulatory* divergence from confounding batch/timing/trans effects, quantifying that **~39%** of human–chimp cortical expression divergence is cis.
- **Independent, mechanistic corroboration of neoteny/heterochrony** central to the manuscript: a slower human neurogenesis→astrogenesis transition, and cis-encoded upregulation of a human **astrocyte** gene module under apparent selection — relevant given the emerging role of glia in ASD and human cortical specialization.
- **Interprets human-specific vulnerability.** 100 ASE genes overlap neurodevelopmental-disorder gene sets (incl. **SCN1A**, **GRIN2A**), directly supporting the idea that human-specific *regulatory* changes can be substrates for both human cortical function and disease susceptibility — a framing usable in the ASD review's discussion of why human cortex is selectively vulnerable.
- **From regulation to physiology.** SSTR2 shows that a purely *cis-regulatory* (protein-identical) human-specific change produces a **human-specific neuronal calcium/excitability phenotype** — a template for arguing that ASD-relevant human-specific circuit properties may arise from gene-regulatory, not coding, divergence.
- **Complementary tool to organoid perturbation.** Fusion controls variability that plagues cross-species (and potentially case/control) organoid comparisons; the authors explicitly propose fusing **patient vs control** lines to disentangle polygenic disease aetiology while controlling batch — a candidate design for future ASD organoid studies.

## Open questions / limitations
- **Single developmental snapshot for scRNA-seq** (day 150, 2 lines, 368–706 cells) — limited cell-type/temporal resolution; trajectories inferred, not longitudinally tracked.
- **Non-neural contamination:** Matrigel embedding introduced mesenchymal/epithelial cells; a chimp-biased mesenchymal cell-fate difference complicates parental comparisons (mitigated by hybrid ASE, which showed no cis fate bias).
- **Small line panel:** 5 hybrid lines from only 2 parental pairs; 3 human + 3 chimp parents — limits population-genetic generalization; cannot distinguish human-upregulation vs chimp-downregulation for the directional selection signal without outgroup (macaque) allelic data.
- **Aneuploidy/culture artefacts** (recurrent chr20q gain; chr18/20 gains) required excluding whole chromosomes from analysis.
- **Tetraploid state** could in principle perturb dosage-sensitive regulation, though authors argue effects are minimal (species profiles preserved; ~40% of ASE is differentiation-specific rather than ploidy-driven).
- **Ethical constraints:** explicitly not for in vivo/organismal use; authors recommend bioethics consultation for future applications.
- **SSTR2 mechanism** shown pharmacologically (antagonist CYN) — the causal chain from cis-regulatory variant → expression → calcium phenotype is correlative at the variant level (specific enhancer not mapped here).
